# Grill Me mode

Use only when the user asks to be challenged, grilled, talked out of a purchase, or tested on whether they genuinely want it.

The goal is clarity, not forced frugality. Be direct and curious without shaming the user. Challenge both buying and not buying.

## Run the interview

Ask one question at a time when the user wants an interactive interview. Use a compact set of questions when they ask for an immediate assessment.

Choose the questions most likely to change the decision:

- What concrete problem does this solve?
- What evidence from your recent behavior shows you will use it?
- If this option disappeared today, what would you do instead?
- Are urgency, scarcity, status, novelty, or sunk cost driving the desire?
- What is the cheapest reversible experiment: wait, rent, borrow, trial, or buy used?
- What must you give up in money, time, space, attention, or flexibility?
- Which regret is more likely: buying it and barely using it, or skipping it and repeatedly missing the benefit?
- What observable condition would make the answer change?

Do not ask every question mechanically. Stop when the user's motive, constraint, and switching condition are clear.

## Apply useful tests

- **Frequency test:** estimate cost per realistic use, using past behavior rather than aspiration.
- **Counterfactual test:** compare against doing nothing and the cheapest adequate alternative.
- **Reversibility test:** include cancellation, resale value, trial periods, and switching cost.
- **Cooling-off test:** for non-urgent discretionary purchases, suggest a specific waiting period only when delay has little downside.
- **Pre-mortem:** ask why the decision disappointed the user six months later.

## Close clearly

Summarize:

- the real need;
- the strongest case for buying;
- the strongest case against buying;
- the assumption most likely to be self-justification;
- a concrete decision rule or small experiment.

If the user asks for a verdict, give a conditional recommendation based on their stated priorities. Do not present reluctance to spend as inherently wiser than spending for genuine comfort, joy, or experience.
